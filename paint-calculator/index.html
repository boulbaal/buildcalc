    How it works

    The calculator measures the wall surface around the room, subtracts a standard area for each door and window, optionally adds the ceiling, multiplies by the number of coats, and divides by your paint's coverage. The result is always rounded up to whole cans, because you cannot buy 1.8 gallons:

    wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height
    paintable = wall area − doors × 21 − windows × 15 (+ ceiling)
    gallons = paintable × coats ÷ coverage, rounded up

    In metric mode a door counts as 1.95 m², a window as 1.4 m², and coverage is per liter.

    Worked example

    Say your room is 12 × 10 ft with 8 ft walls, one door and one window, and you plan two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon. The wall area is 2 × (12 + 10) × 8 = 352 sq ft. Subtract the door (21 sq ft) and the window (15 sq ft) to get 316 sq ft per coat. Two coats means 632 sq ft of coverage, and 632 ÷ 350 = 1.81, which rounds up to 2 gallons. Tick "Include ceiling" and the 120 sq ft ceiling brings it to 436 sq ft per coat, 872 sq ft total, so you need 3 gallons.

    FAQ

    How much does a gallon of paint cover?

    Most interior paints cover 250 to 400 square feet per gallon on a smooth, primed wall. This calculator defaults to 350. Rough, porous or previously unpainted surfaces absorb more paint, so use the low end of the range for textured drywall, bare wood or masonry.

    Do I need primer before painting?

    Use primer on bare drywall, bare wood, stained or glossy surfaces, and when covering a dark color with a lighter one. On previously painted walls in good condition and a similar color, a quality paint-and-primer product is usually enough. Primer is cheaper than an extra coat of paint.

    Is one coat of paint enough, or do I need two?

    Plan on two coats for an even, durable finish. One coat can work when repainting with the same color and sheen on a well-prepared wall. Big color changes, reds and yellows, or painting over patches often need two coats plus a tinted primer underneath.

    How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

    A 12 by 12 foot room with 8 foot walls has about 384 square feet of wall. After subtracting a door and a window you paint roughly 348 square feet per coat, so two coats need about 696 square feet of coverage, which is 2 gallons at 350 square feet per gallon.

    Is ceiling paint different from wall paint?

    Ceiling paint is formulated thicker so it splatters and drips less, and it is almost always flat so light does not highlight roller marks. You can use flat wall paint on a ceiling, but a dedicated ceiling paint is easier to apply overhead and hides imperfections better.
