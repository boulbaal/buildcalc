    How it works

    The calculator divides your area by the footprint of one tile plus its grout gap, adds a waste allowance for cuts and breakage, and rounds up to whole tiles:

    effective tile area = (tile width + gap) × (tile height + gap)
    tiles = ceil( area / effective tile area × (1 + waste) )
    boxes = ceil( tiles / tiles per box )

    Waste is 10% for a straight grid layout and 15% for diagonal or herringbone, where more offcuts end up unusable. A wider grout gap means each tile covers a slightly larger footprint, so the tile count goes down a little.

    Worked example

    Say you are tiling a 10 ft by 12 ft floor, which is 120 sq ft, with 12 x 12 inch tiles laid straight and no grout gap. Each tile covers 144 sq in, exactly 1 sq ft, so bare coverage is 120 / 1 = 120 tiles. Add 10% waste: 120 × 1.1 = 132, so you should buy 132 tiles. If the tiles come 8 to a box, that is ceil(132 / 8) = 17 boxes. The same floor laid diagonally at 15% waste needs 138 tiles.

    FAQ

    How much extra tile should I buy?

    Buy 10-15% more than the bare coverage number. Cuts along walls, breakage and future repairs all eat into the extra. Use 10% for a simple straight layout in a rectangular room, and 15% for diagonal or herringbone layouts, small tiles, or rooms with many corners.

    What is tile waste?

    Waste is the tile you buy but do not end up walking on: offcuts at walls and around fixtures, tiles that crack during cutting or transport, and spares kept for later repairs. It is normal, not a mistake. This calculator adds the waste percentage before rounding up to whole tiles.

    How many 12x12 tiles do I need for 100 square feet?

    A 12x12 inch tile covers exactly 1 square foot, so 100 square feet needs 100 tiles of bare coverage. Add 10% waste for a straight layout and you should buy 110 tiles. With a grout gap each tile effectively covers slightly more, so the count can drop a little.

    Does a diagonal layout need more tile?

    Yes. Laying tiles at 45 degrees means every row ends in an angled cut at the wall, and many of those offcuts cannot be reused. Plan on about 15% waste for diagonal or herringbone layouts instead of the usual 10% for a straight grid layout.

    Do I count grout lines when calculating tile?

    Yes, and they work in your favor. Each tile plus its grout gap covers a slightly larger footprint than the tile alone, so a wider gap slightly reduces the number of tiles needed. The effect is small with large tiles but noticeable with small tiles like subway tile.
